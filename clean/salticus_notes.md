# Observations on the Salticus scenicus contamination in SE samples
 - See results from running the script `salticus.R`
 - The only Salticus species occurring in controls is S. scenicus. It occurs only in buffer blanks and extraction negs, never in pcr negs. 
 - S. scenicus is only represented by one BOLD bin in controls, BOLD:AAC9044, and 23 unique ASV IDs (as dominant in the sample). The most common dominant ASV is bf49f50fa8ede27f04ee2793202b7057, with others only dominant in single samples, or up to 4 samples.
 - In samples, S. scenicus is represented by 249 different dominant ASVs in 2465 samples. Only 44 samples have more than 100 reads of the dominant S. scenicus ASV, and all of them have one of the dominant ASVs found in controls except one. This unusual sample has the dominant ASV being 6084ea09aefde67756e4b9b0aacf29e9, which is never found in controls as the dominant ASV. Possibly, this could indicate an authentic occurrence of S. scenicus, but it seems questionable. More than 1000 reads of the dominant ASV are found in 19 samples; they seem likely to contain specimens of S. scenicus but the ASV is identical to the dominant one found in the controls.
 - There are max 120 reads of S. scenicus (dominant ASV) in the controls.
 - The S. scenicus contamination occurs in half or more of the buffer blanks and extraction negs in batch04 to batch11 (slightly lower in batch10), only in one buffer blank of 10 in batch18. Thus, the contamination is focused in time but it also coincides with the Swedish lysate batches. There are no Salticus scenicus records from Madagascar (lysate sample batches pre-dating batch18 but post-dating batch11).
 - About half of the field samples in batch04 to batch11 contain S. scenicus (same fraction as controls); lower fractions in controls and field samples in batch10. From batch 16, there are few samples with S. scenicus (but not zero).
 - The relative proportion of trace DNA (< 100 reads) to non-trace DNA (>100 reads) varies across batches as follows (high-read samples:low-read samples): batch04 9:361, batch05 2:247, batch06 6:410, batch07 3:330, batch08 5:323, batch09 6:355, batch10 1:111, batch11 7:253, batch16 0:4, batch17 3:7, batch18 2:20. Thus, significantly higher fraction of high-read samples in batch17 and batch18, too few samples in batch16 to form a definite conclusion.
 - Half or more (44% to 65%) of the lysate samples in batch04 to batch11 contain S. scenicus, except for batch10 when the proportion is slightly lower (22%). The homogenates have many fewer samples with S. scenicus (batch 16 to batch18), 2.6 to 4.8%. 

# Interpretations from Salticus patterns
 - The Salticus analysis suggests that it is better to remove clusters and not single ASVs, otherwise likely contaminants that represent unusual sequences are not removed effectively.
 - The relative proportion of trace DNA in samples, and the relative proportion of samples with Salticus DNA suggests that batchwise removal of contaminants would be better. Then likely correct records would be retained in batch16, batch17 and batch18 (depending on threshold for contamination, batch10 has lowest frequency 5:15, 1:4 for buffer blanks and extraction negs, respectively, except for batch18, which has 1:10 and 0:8, respectively, and batch 16 and 17 (no Salticus contamination)).
